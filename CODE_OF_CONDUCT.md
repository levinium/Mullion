# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are acting in good faith. Criticise code, not
the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- **Keep it about the work.** Disagreement about a design, a bug or a decision is
  normal and welcome. Disagreement about a person is not.
- **Assume good faith.** Most confusing behaviour is a misunderstanding, a
  language difference, or someone having a bad day.
- **Take correction plainly.** Being wrong about something is ordinary. Saying so
  and moving on costs nothing.
- **Respect the maintainer's time and yours.** This is a single-maintainer
  project worked on in spare hours. A feature may be declined, a pull request may
  wait, and an issue may be closed as out of scope. None of that is a judgement
  of you.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about any personal characteristic —
  including age, disability, appearance, ethnicity, gender identity or
  expression, nationality, race, religion, or sexual identity or orientation.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing other people's private information without their permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or persistently ignoring reasonable
  requests to stop.

## Where this applies

Anywhere the project is being represented or discussed: issues, pull requests,
discussions, commit messages, and any other project space.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately to the maintainer, [@levinium](https://github.com/levinium),
through
[GitHub's private reporting](https://github.com/levinium/Mullion/security/advisories/new)
— it is intended for security issues, but it is the only private channel this
project has, and it works for this too.

Reports are taken seriously and kept confidential. Because this is a
single-maintainer project, a report about the maintainer has nowhere impartial to
go, which is worth saying plainly rather than pretending otherwise; GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) exists for that case.

## Consequences

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, close issues or pull requests, and
block accounts, where conduct falls short of the above. Severity and repetition
both matter: a single sharp comment is not the same as a pattern, and will not be
treated as one.
